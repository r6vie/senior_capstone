What it does, and where it falls short

What is on it

  • The book as an object: a plain white cover with the title and name, a status chip, the title, “by”, the back cover, and “Edit the title and back cover”.
  • One next step that changes with the book’s stage: Add to your book (with Undo), Review your book, Continue adding, Try again, Review the proposed changes, Finish removing, Put back on the shelf.
  • Chapters: number, title, one line, then Edit, Show what you said, Remove this chapter.
  • A question that came from a reading, with Answer and No thanks.
  • “For your next questions”: the sparks, each with Remove.
  • Take off the shelf, with a confirm in place.

What works

  • The book is the one focal point and there are no numbers anywhere.
  • The next step is always one red button.
  • Everything the member can change is on the page, in place.
  • The phone order puts the question before the chapters.

What is missing or weak

  • The cover is blank. There is no way to pick a design, and the page does not show the book the way the shelf does.
  • The Librarian is absent. “Add to your book” is a plain button. Nothing on the page shows what the Librarian kept, or what it wants to ask next.
  • Three jobs share one column (look at the book, edit it, continue it), so the red button sits beside a stack of small grey links and three helper sentences.
  • The aside is a pile. A reading question and the sparks are the same idea (things that shape the next questions) in two different boxes.
  • It is still the old look: square, flat, dotted rules, no depth. It does not match the Folio shelf.
