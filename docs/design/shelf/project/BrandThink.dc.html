Brand · round 1 · the thinking

Who Trove is, before what it looks like

Mission first, then the feeling, then twelve ideas for a mark and an honest note on each. Three survive; each has its own board to the right.

The missionFrom docs/trove.md
You are the author of the book you haven't written yet.

Trove helps CWRU students and alumni meet. An interview finds what each member knows that others don't and turns it into a book: a title and chapters, all in their own words. Other members browse the shelf and check a book out, which means a short video call with its author.

It is deliberately not LinkedIn. No feed, no followers, no counts, no polish. A first-year's book and a VP's book look identical on the shelf. Meeting comes first; opportunities follow from people who were curious about each other.

What any mark must do
Ink, never redRed is the one action on each screen. If the logo were red, it would be a second button.
Work at 16pxA browser tab and a phone icon first, a poster second.
Say people, not statusNo crests, ranks, charts or trophies. Nothing that suggests who is ahead.
Belong to the systemInk on paper, square corners, dot tone for shade. The covers bring the colour.
Twelve ideasSketched, then judged against the mission
Open bookCut

The plain sign for a book.

Every library, e-reader and course site already owns it. It says books, not people.

Treasure chestCut

Read “trove” literally: a hoard of treasure.

Pirates and loot boxes. Treasure you own is status, the opposite of lending what you know.

Key and keyholeCut

Unlock what is in you.

A cliché, and it reads as security or a password manager.

QuillCut

The author's pen.

Antique, and it suggests the member writes. The interview writes; the member talks.

Spines on a shelfCut

Members as books standing side by side.

Uneven spines read as a bar chart: a ranking. Trove has no counts, so its mark can't look like one.

Speech bubble bookCut

A book you talk with.

Two ideas glued together; it lands as a chat app, and Trove has no cold messages.

Hidden gemCut

Everyone is a hidden gem; dot tone as the facets.

Jewellery and crypto. Also a compliment, and compliments are a kind of rating.

Due-date stampCut

The library stamp you get when you check out.

Warm and true to checkouts, but it is about borrowing, not authorship, and it slides into retro pastiche.

Collegiate monogramCut

A crest, because it starts at CWRU.

Crests are about rank and belonging to the right club. Trove is a level shelf; a VP and a first-year sit side by side.

Bookmark TKeep · A

A ribbon hanging from the edge of a book. It holds a place, and it is the T.

Simple enough for a 16px tab, and it names a quiet act: holding a place in someone's book.

Facing pagesKeep · B

An open book whose two pages are two profiles, face to face. The spine is the space between them.

The whole product in one shape: a book that is a conversation. Needs careful drawing so the faces read small.

The blank coverKeep · C

Trove's own book object, outline and dot shadow, with the title not written yet.

Comes straight out of the screentone system, and it says the tagline: the book you haven't written yet.

The three we keepEach answers the mission from a different side
A · The actBookmark

Marking someone's book to come back to. Quiet, literary, works at every size.

B · The meetingFacing pages

A book that is two people talking. The warmest and most ownable of the three.

C · The promiseThe blank cover

The book you haven't written yet. The most native to the screentone system we already have.
