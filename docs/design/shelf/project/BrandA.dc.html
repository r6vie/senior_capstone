Bookmark

A ribbon hanging from the edge of a book: it holds a place, and it is the T.

A bookmark holds the place where reading stops and talking starts. The mark is the quietest of the three and the most literary: no illustration, just an edge and a ribbon, and it holds at 16px. Its risk is that it is calm enough to be forgettable, so the marketing leans on the ribbon hanging into the frame as the brand's one gesture.

ConstructionHow it is drawn and why
  • The edgeA book's edge seen from below: one bar, 76 wide, 13 high. It is also the T's arm.
  • The ribbon18 wide, hanging 57, with a swallowtail cut 9.5 deep: the ribbon you leave where a conversation should start. It is also the T's stem.
  • The wordmarkTrove in Newsreader 600, the face of every book title, set on a rule (the book's foot) with the same ribbon slipping out under the v. Use the full wordmark wherever there's room; the mark alone for tabs, icons and avatars.
  • NeverColour the ribbon red, tilt it, animate it swinging, or add a second ribbon.
