The Magpie

Trove as the publisher of books nobody has written yet, with a publisher's colophon: a magpie standing on a closed book.

Every press has its animal on the spine. A magpie gathers bright things into one place, is curious, and talks, which is the trove, the reading and the checkout in one bird. It is drawn like a linocut: solid ink, white cut out for the belly and shoulder, one cut line along the wing.

Trove
Why a magpieEvery publisher has a colophon; Trove publishes the books nobody has written yet

It keeps a trove.Magpies are known for gathering bright things into one place. That is the shelf: what each member knows, kept where others can find it.

It is curious and it talks.One of the cleverest birds, and a noisy, social one. Curiosity and conversation are the whole product.

It is black and white.The ink-and-paper look stays, with one hidden colour: the blue-green sheen a magpie’s tail shows in the light. The thief story is folklore; the real bird keeps what it finds interesting, which is the point.
