Getting started · the reveal

From the last answer to the shelf: three white books get their titles, you take one off the shelf, it opens for review, and on the shelf it finally gets its cover art.

Needs Josef’s yes
  • No black band. The Librarian’s line sits across the top of the page under a small “The Librarian” label (left off when the line already names it), in the same large serif as the question on the interview; lines that wait (finding, writing) are italic and grey. Every interview screen then has the same shape: top bar, one line across the top, the content.
  • The books will be animated (the titles arriving, the pick, the book opening, going onto the shelf). Each step’s motion is specified in its notes, and the landing under step 7; these are the still frames between them.
  • The three titles are white books on a shelf, and the whole book is what you click; the chosen one lifts and gets a bookmark (replaces cards with radio buttons).
  • Review uses the shelf’s open book, as on My book D2: fields by default and no inline chapter actions. Rewrite chapter and Rewrite back cover are removed (Josef, 2026-11-23); Remove stays, on the chapter’s page.
  • The done screen shows the book in its finished cover art on the shelf. This assumes the design is set when the book goes on the shelf; if the member is meant to choose a design here first, this step changes.
  • The back cover is the last entry in the contents, after the chapters, and opens like a chapter, as on My book (Josef).

Review is the shelf’s open book

The book you review is the same open book as My book: the title is a field from the start, with the Librarian’s signoff under your name, and the back cover is the last entry in the contents, opening like a chapter; the contents have no dividers, and a chapter opens to its own title and text as fields. Everything saves as you type.

Put it on the shelf is the one red button, under the book, with See the other titles beside it.

Motion. As the book is written, the contents fill row by row: each chapter’s title types into its place and its summary fades in under it. When the last one lands, the title page’s fields become editable. With reduced motion every step is a short crossfade.

The payoff: the cover gets its art

The book stands on the real shelf among other members’ books, now in its finished design, pulled slightly forward. The neighbours sit under a light dot tone. The words and the two actions are today’s.

The landing. Put it on the shelf closes the open book, its cover swinging shut about the spine as on the shelf. The closed book, still white, turns to face you and travels down to the shelf. Its neighbours slide apart to open a gap, and the book settles into it on one soft ease-out, with no bounce and no overshoot; its dot shadow appears as it touches the shelf (about 1.6 seconds). Then its cover arrives on the fast timing, about 4 seconds: a watercolour cover paints itself in, a strata cover builds itself from the bottom up, each slab settling into place (Josef, 2026-11-23; the slow painting is only for the sign-in intro). The art session’s board StudioPaint has both. With reduced motion it is a short crossfade to this frame.
