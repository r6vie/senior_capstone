Facing pages

An open book whose two pages are two people face to face; the spine is the space between them.

Trove's whole idea in one shape: a book you read by talking to its author. It is the warmest and most ownable of the three, and the only one that shows two people as equals. Its risk is drawing: the profiles must read at 32px and stay dignified, never cartoonish. It also leans on a well-known figure-ground trick, so the profiles have to be ours.

ConstructionHow it is drawn and why
  • Two pagesAn open book seen from the front, the outer corners low and the heads rising to the gutter.
  • Two peopleThe inner edge of each page is one profile: brow, nose, lips, chin. The right page is the left mirrored, so neither speaker is first. The spine is the space between them.
  • The gapAt the noses the pages come within 4 of 120: close enough to read as talking, never touching.
  • The wordmarktrove in Newsreader italic 500, lowercase: the Librarian's voice, said rather than announced.
  • NeverGive the faces eyes, colour one page differently, or put a face on only one side.
