Logo · five options

A new logo for Trove

Twelve ideas were considered against the mission, the feel and the job a logo has to do in a product. Five survive. Each is shown as a lockup, in the app header, on black, as a mark at three sizes, and as an icon and avatar. They are ink only.

What the logo has to do

Mission

Trove is where a member finds what others at CWRU know that they don’t. Every member is the author of the book they haven’t written yet; others browse the books and check one out, which is a short video call with the author. The logo has to say book and person without saying “social network”.

Feel

Printed matter: ink on paper, calm and exact, warm without being cute. Professional enough to sit next to a résumé, not a game and not a corporation. Nothing performative: no sparkle, no badge, no gradient. It has to feel like it was made by someone who reads.

Function

It has to work as a 16px favicon, a 26px app-header lockup, a round avatar and an app icon; in one colour, on paper and on black. It sits next to Barlow Condensed caps, so it is drawn in that voice. Red stays for the one action, so the logo is ink only.

The five, in order of how far they go from the current wordmark

A

Ribbon

The T of Trove is a bookmark: a crossbar with a notched ribbon hanging from it.

Why it fits. A bookmark marks the place you want to come back to, which is what a member’s thread is, and it is the same notched ribbon the interface already uses for “the one thing waiting for you”. The notch is the only detail, so it survives at 16px.

The risk. The least literal of the five, so it has to be learned once. A bookmark alone can read as “saved”.

Open V

The V of Trove is an open book: two pages meeting at the gutter.

Why it fits. The premise in one letter: a person opening up. It sits in the middle of the word, so it reads as a letter first and a book second, and the gutter is a single cut.

The risk. Weakest of the five. The gutter closes below about 24px, leaving a plain V, and a V with a crack can read as a broken letter. It earns its place only if the idea matters more than the favicon.

The book

The product’s own object: the book with a hinge, a T on its cover and the dot shadow under it.

Why it fits. It is exactly the book the member sees everywhere, so the logo and the interface are one object. The dot shadow is the system’s signature; below 48px the mark drops the shadow and the hinge and keeps a bold T on a solid cover.

The risk. The plainest. A book is also what a thousand reading apps use, so it leans on the dots and the T to be ours.

Stamp

A library date stamp: “Trove” above, “Checked out” below, a book in the middle.

Why it fits. Check-out is the one verb the product owns, and a stamp is the physical trace of it. It is the only round mark, so it is the one that works as an avatar, a social image and a seal.

The risk. The busiest. The ring text is for large sizes only; below about 48px it becomes a thick ring around the book. It is a seal, not a favicon.

What an independent critic found, and what changed

Lockups for D and E clippedFixed. All five wordmarks are now drawn from the same letters, so none depends on a loaded font. Only the stamp’s ring text is live type, and it is pinned to a fixed length.
Letter spacing and weightFixed. T–R is 5 (was 10), R–O is 8 (was 14), V–E is 3. The V’s arms are 27 wide (was 25), the O is wider to match the R’s bowl, and the E’s counters are 20 high (were 17).
Stacked T: page lines were painted, not cutFixed. The lines are real cut-outs 6 in from the edge on both books, and the bar is 24 thick like the other letters. It now works in one colour on any ground.
Ribbon: notch overshot the baselineFixed. The tips sit on the baseline and the apex is 16 up.
Stamp: ring text hit both ringsFixed. The band is wider, the text is smaller and sits inside it. Below about 48px the stamp is a thick ring around the book.
The book: no small master, misaligned lockupFixed. The book now spans the cap height and its shadow ends on the baseline. Below 48px it loses the shadow and hinge and has a bold T.
Open V: the gutter cut empty airFixed. It starts at the V’s inner vertex and is 8 wide. The critic still ranks it last and recommends dropping it.
Ranking, from the critic1 Ribbon, 2 The book, 3 Stacked T, 4 Stamp, 5 Open V. For the website header, the Ribbon. For the favicon and app icon, also the Ribbon.

Gem or treasure chestCut. “Trove” literally, but a treasure map is a game, and the product is a professional network.
Head-and-shoulders inside a bookCut. It is the premise, but it reads as a default avatar icon and looks like every networking app.
Bar-chart bookshelfCut. Books of different heights read as analytics.
Halftone TCut. The dots are the system’s signature, but dots fall apart at favicon size.
Sunburst behind a bookCut. The burst belongs to the Librarian; a logo with it reads as a sunrise or a faith.
KeyholeCut. Hidden and secret; the product is open and warm.
Quill or pen nibCut. Old-fashioned, and writing is the Librarian’s act, not the member’s.
