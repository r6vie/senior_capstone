# The strata critic: Josef's eye

A brief for any critic reviewing strata covers and book pages. Judge as Josef does. Each point below is something he
has actually rejected or asked for while this collection was made (Nov 2026). Push for perfection: a first draft
he would call "not there yet" is a failure, not a pass.

## Composition
- One clear focal point, and a balanced picture. Several dark verticals of similar weight at even intervals read as
  a picket fence. Weight piled in two opposite corners tips like a seesaw.
- Layering: a back plane, a middle plane and a near plane that step toward the viewer, with a foreground element
  that anchors a corner.
- Avoid tangents: the sun or a peak shouldn't just kiss another shape's edge, and a spire shouldn't cut the sun.
- Leading lines (a river, a road, a wash) should lead to the focal point.
- Not too much foreground. Zoom in when the scene feels distant.

## Shapes
- Hills must not look like eggs, igloos or domes, and peaks must not look like pyramids or towers. Flanks spread into
  the ground. Profiles are uneven, with shoulders and secondary summits.
- Edges step, but a long slope's profile should run as a smooth diagonal, not a staircase.
- Things sit on the ground. A floating hut, tree, palm or bush is an instant fail.
- Nothing repetitive or uniform. Even stripes read as "cake" or lined paper. Vary sizes, spacing and rhythm, and
  never line objects up in a perfect row.

## Value and colour
- There must be a value range with a dark anchor. Thin, pale, bland work is a fail.
- A shape must separate from what's behind it: a white roof on white snow is invisible.
- Light comes from the left: lit left faces, shaded right faces, cast shadows thrown right.
- Muted, cohesive inks. No colour that could pass for the UI red (#D42A27), so barns and bridges are rust or brown,
  never red. No ugly yellow streaks in water.

## Cleanliness
- No white gaps or white lines inside the art. Water must fill to the edges, with no paper between planes.
- No stray floating specks, and no weird spikes or wedges.
- A dissolve into paper must be gradual, made with gaps and omitted slabs, never a hard cut.

## Craft
- Small objects (buildings, boats, bridges) must read at a glance, built from a few solid forms with care. They must
  not look demolished or noisy.
- A cover is minimal; its book page is a much richer scene.
- Every cover must be original. It must not reuse another cover's composition with a new texture.

## Hard layout rule
- On the 236 x 344 world cover, at shelf size, a three-line title plus a one-line name reaches world y ~171,
  left of x ~200. Left of x ~200, the art stays below y ~180. Only the right edge may rise higher.
- On a book page, the text holds the upper left (about x 0-470 and y 0-350 of 570 x 684), so the art climbs on
  the right.

## Relatability
- The collection needs places most American members would pick for their own book: recognisable, warm, inviting.
